Responsible Business

Anti-Slavery and Human Trafficking Statement

PT Kapan Kita Bersama, trading as myBalivisa, does not tolerate slavery, forced labour, human trafficking, debt bondage, servitude, child exploitation or other forms of coercive labour in our business or business relationships.

Last updated: 3 October 2026

01

Our Standard

We conduct our business in accordance with applicable Indonesian law.

We also support internationally recognised principles concerning human rights, decent work, forced labour and child labour.

No person working for us or providing services to us should:

  • be forced or coerced to work;
  • be required to surrender a passport or identity document as a condition of work;
  • be subject to threats, violence, intimidation or restriction of movement;
  • be charged recruitment fees that create or contribute to debt bondage;
  • have wages unlawfully withheld;
  • be prevented from freely ending their employment, subject to lawful contractual requirements;
  • be subjected to deceptive recruitment or materially misleading employment terms; or
  • be subjected to slavery, servitude, trafficking or other forms of labour exploitation.

02

Child Labour

We do not use unlawful child labour.

We require employment involving young persons to comply with Indonesian minimum-age, education, working-time, health and safety, and prohibited-work requirements.

We do not tolerate the use of children in slavery, trafficking, forced labour or other forms of work prohibited by law.

03

Employees and Recruitment

Employment must be voluntary.

Workers must receive clear information about the material terms of their employment.

We do not use recruitment practices designed to place workers into debt or make them dependent on an employer, recruiter or intermediary.

Personal identity documents remain under the control of their owner except where temporary possession is reasonably necessary for a lawful administrative process and the document is returned promptly.

04

Suppliers and Business Partners

We expect contractors, suppliers, agents and other business partners involved in providing our services to comply with applicable labour and human-rights laws.

We can conduct additional enquiries where a business relationship, activity, location or workforce presents an increased risk of forced labour, trafficking or worker exploitation.

Where a material concern is identified, we can require corrective action, reconsider the business relationship, or report suspected unlawful conduct to the appropriate authority.

05

Identifying Risk

We use recognised indicators of forced labour as practical warning signs.

These can include deception, abuse of vulnerability, restriction of movement, isolation, threats, violence, retention of identity documents, withholding of wages, debt bondage, abusive working or living conditions, and excessive involuntary overtime.

The presence of a warning sign requires appropriate review. It does not by itself establish that forced labour has occurred.

06

Reporting Concerns

Employees, clients, contractors and other persons can raise concerns about suspected forced labour, trafficking or exploitation.

We will assess credible concerns and take appropriate action based on the circumstances and applicable law.

A person who raises a genuine concern should not be subjected to retaliation for making the report in good faith.

07

Legal and International Framework

Our approach is informed by applicable Indonesian law and recognised international labour and human-rights standards.

These include Indonesian legislation concerning human rights, employment, human trafficking and child labour, together with relevant International Labour Organization conventions ratified by Indonesia.

We also take account of internationally recognised responsible business principles, including the United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights.

08

Review

We review this statement and our related controls periodically and when there is a material change to our operations or applicable legal requirements.
